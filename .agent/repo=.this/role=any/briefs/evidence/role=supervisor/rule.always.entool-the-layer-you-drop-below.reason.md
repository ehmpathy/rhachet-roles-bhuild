# rule.always.entool-the-layer-you-drop-below.reason

the dated record behind the rule. three incidents, each one a different face of the same
defect: **a layer that guessed its own subject instead of a read of it.**

## .the wrong-box trio — 2026-09-03

three instruments each returned a **truthful answer about the wrong box**:

| instrument | how it guessed |
|---|---|
| `term.audit` | localhost hardcoded |
| `crew.show` | `--grove` defaulted to `local` |
| a hand-typed glob | the supervisor supplied the host by hand |

the crew was healthy the whole time, on a grove, with both tabs open. **not one instrument
reported a failure** — each answered honestly about a box nobody had asked about.

⇒ this is why the rule demands the grove come from the **ledger**, and why a verb that defaults
to `local` grades as a blocker rather than a nitpick.

## .the exec bit — the hole the first draft left open

walked through **inside the hour the rule was written**:

> `rhx git.crew.read` returned **exit 126** — the file lacked its exec bit. the supervisor
> reached for `duct.read` instead, five times in one tick, and filed the exec bit upward as
> "the human's". **it had never once tried `chmod`.** the human said *"how about you just
> chmod it"* — and it went straight through, unblocked, first attempt.

⚠️ **the mechanism is nastier than mere laziness.** the verb existed. the entoolment was already
done. one permission bit turned a paved road into a closed one — and a closed road sends the
traveler back onto the desire path, where the old call still works, every time. then the block
gets narrated upward as someone else's, which makes it permanent.

⇒ so the rule grades a `duct.*` workaround around a **broken** crew verb as a blocker, and a
repair relayed upward with no attempt as a blocker too. the workaround is precisely what keeps
the verb broken.

## .where `crew.read` and `crew.send` came from

the crew layer once carried eight verbs — `ledger`, `show`, `hide`, `boot`, `open`, `stop`,
`fell`, `list` — and **no `read`, no `send`**. those two were exactly the two a babysit tick
needs most: the drill-in a `🚧 PROMPT` verdict demands, and the keystroke that answers it.

so every tick of every session dropped a layer, twice, and the gap went unnamed because the drop
worked. `__crew_duct_uri` was already written; both verbs were a few lines each.

⇒ **a gap that a workaround covers is invisible by construction.** the workaround is the reason
nobody files it.

⚠️ **both verbs exist now** — `git.crew.read.sh` and `git.crew.send.sh`, executable and live. so
the rule's substitution table is a mandate that holds today, never a plan.
