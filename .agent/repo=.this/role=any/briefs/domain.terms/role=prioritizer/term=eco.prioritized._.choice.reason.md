# domain.term.choice.reason: prioritized

## .etymology

from **priority** — the store's own root noun (`eco.priority`). `prioritized` is the
adjectival state of a row that holds an opine: the org has judged it worth a place in the
queue, at a stated sev·urg. the word inherits the store's name on purpose, so the WORTH half
of a row carries the same root as the concept it grades.

⇒ so `prioritized` reads as *"the org wants it, and said how badly"*, in contrast to
`sponsored`, which reads *"a human put budget on it"*.

## .why the word was needed

`sponsored` could not be defined alone. a reader who sees a ⭐ asks *"above WHAT?"*, and the
answer is *"above the merely prioritized rows"*. the two words are a contrast pair
(`rule.prefer.symmetric-term-pairs`), and a contrast needs both poles named — a ⭐ with no
word for its opposite invites the misread that a sponsored row is *"more important"* rather
than *"funded now"*.

## .why not the rejected words

| rejected | why |
|---|---|
| `wanted` | too vague, and it drops the GRADE. every row is wanted; `prioritized` says the org also stated how badly (sev) and by when (urg) |
| `important` | names severity alone, and drops urgency. a p3·1d and a p0·1m are both prioritized, at opposite ends of the two axes |
| `ranked` | names the OUTPUT (the sort order), not the state of a row. a row is prioritized before any rank runs; the rank is what reads the opine |
| `graded` / `rated` | imply a scale handed down from outside. the opine is chosen by the person at the keyboard, not assigned by an external grader (the same reason `grade` was rejected for `opine`) |
| `weighted` | invites a reader to treat sev·urg as a tunable numeric weight, which is exactly the false precision the opine refuses (`rule.forbid.sev-from-cash-alone`) |

🔴 **`ranked` is the near miss worth the record.** it reads well — a prioritized row does get
ranked — and it fails because it names the DERIVED order rather than the STATE that produces
it. a row is prioritized whether or not a rank has run; the rank is a read of the opine, never
the opine itself. to name the state after its output is the same error as `starred` for
`sponsored`.

## .evidence

### the discovery — the wisher's own split, 2026-09-13

> "lets enbrief the distinction between prioritized vs sponsored; prioritized is the org wants
> it. sponsored is some human has authorized their budget on it (time review, cash tokens +
> compute)"

the wisher named both words in one sentence and assigned each its sense. `prioritized` = the
org wants it; `sponsored` = a human authorized budget. the pair is the deliverable, not either
word alone.

### the boundary

`eco`. "prioritized, of WHAT?" → of an `eco.priority`. one word, so
`rule.require.boundary-qualified-terms` is satisfied at authorship rather than after a
collision.
