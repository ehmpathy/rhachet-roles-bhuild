# domain.term.choice.reason: subversive

## .etymology

### `subversive` — the wisher's word, adopted rather than translated

the term entered mid-drive, in the steer that authored the rule:

> ruthlessly wick and cut to the bone the yield. eliminate any subversive prose. if its not a
> disputable statement, then it didnt say shit

⇒ the plain sense fits: *subversive* = **seeks to undermine**. and that is the discriminator against
every candidate below — an unsettleable sentence does not merely waste space, it **takes the slot a
settleable claim was owed**, so the reader leaves with a false sense of a mechanism explained.

`unfalsifiable` was the obvious alternative and is wrong twice: it names the **test** rather than the
defect, and it covers both shapes rather than either.

### `shallow` · `squishy` — ruled by the wisher, 2026-09-06

> *"i preffer shallow and squishy"*
> *"shallow = no substance beneath it. squishy = cant be proven, conforms to whatever you want"*
> *"but maybe shallow -> insubstantial, squishy -> unjustifiable ... as the under translation"*

⇒ **two words per shape, and the wisher's last message settles which is which.**

- the plain word is the definition — a reviewer checks for *insubstantial* and *unjustifiable*
- the short word is the handle — an author recalls *shallow* and *squishy* mid-sentence

`unjustifiable` is barred as the contract word for a reason the one-name principle already covers: in
plain english it also means **morally indefensible**, so it carries two senses. `squishy` carries one.

## .rejected terms

| candidate | for | why not |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| `hollow` | `shallow` | ✅ plain and accurate. `shallow` was preferred as the pair-mate of `squishy` — the two are one register, and a matched pair is recalled as a pair |
| `displacive` | `squishy` | names the **effect** (it displaces a mechanism) rather than the **property** (it cannot be pinned). the effect is downstream of the property, and only the property is checkable at read time |
| `vacuous` · `empty` | `shallow` | accurate, and neither pairs with a word for the second shape |
| `unfalsifiable` | `subversive` | names the test, not the defect. and it covers **both** shapes, so it is the parent rather than a shape |

## .disputes

### dispute: subversive — raised 2026-09-06 — status: RESOLVED (one concept, two shapes)

- raised.by  = beaver
- claim      = 🔴 **`subversive` names two concepts, so a synonym dispute is owed.** sense A, from
               the wisher's steer, is *"if its not a disputable statement, then it didnt say
               shit"* — an empty sentence. sense B, from #358 and #360, is a metaphor, a
               self-issued ✅ verdict, a read-order directive. neither contains the other, so one
               word held two concepts
- counter    = 🔴 **the wisher, verbatim:** *"both a and b are subversive. both are infalsifiable.
               they're just two sub-qualifiers of the subversive concept. no?"*
- resolution = **the split was wrong, and the defect was in ONE WORD of my own test.** i glossed the
               wisher's *"disputable"* as *"could a reader disagree?"* — a test of **taste**, which
               a metaphor passes. their actual test is *"what observation would settle this?"* —
               **falsifiability**, which a metaphor fails. under the right test both shapes fail
               identically, so `subversive` is the parent concept and A/B are its two shapes.
               named `shallow` and `squishy`

🟡 **provenance: the two-sense claim was mine, not the wisher's.** i traced sense A to their steer
and sense B to #358/#360 and concluded they had authored both branches, which put the verdict with
them per `howto.domain-term-disputes`. they had authored one concept; i had mis-tested it.

### the enumeration that proves one question is not enough

one claim, written three ways — *the guard checks reviews at the wrong moment*:

| # | prose | is there a claim? | what observation settles it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *"the review flow has some issues worth a look"* | ❌ | — |
| 2 | *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | ✅ | 🔴 **none** |
| 3 | *"`runStoneGuardReviews` never calls `getStone…Unforgiven`, so the check runs at passage"* | ✅ | ✅ open the file |

⇒ **the test *"is there a claim?"* answers YES on rows 2 and 3.** it sorts row 1 from rows 2–3 and
stops exactly where the second shape begins. the observation test separates all three.

### the settled lesson

> **a test that answers YES on both the good and the bad case is not a test — it is a filter one
> notch too coarse.**

my rule was not wrong. it was **incomplete in a way its own words concealed**: *"could a reader
disagree?"* sounds like a test of quality, and it is a test of **presence**.

⇒ the check, before any rule ships: **write the defect three ways — absent, plausible-but-wrong, and
correct — and confirm the test separates all three.**

🟡 this is `rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name` applied to a **rule's scope** rather than a word,
and that is the sharper case: a wrong word costs a rename; a wrong scope ships a gate that passes the
defect it was built for.

### the term fired on its own author inside 24 hours

measured 2026-09-06, one day after the cluster was written. i verdicted a queue pull-in with:

> *"it falsifies my fail-safe"*

**squishy.** it reads as insight, invites disagreement, and names no mechanism by which the lane in
question raises the objection it claims. the observation test cuts it — and the read that replaced
it **reversed the verdict**: the fail-safe converges argument-shaped objections, which is exactly
what that lane raises.

⇒ **the sentence was not merely empty; it stood where a check belonged, and it deterred me from the
check.** that is the shape `shallow` cannot describe and `squishy` can.
