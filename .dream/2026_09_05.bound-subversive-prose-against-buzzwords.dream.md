## .the gap, precisely

`rule.forbid.subversive-prose` (this route's new rule, per wisher steer) and
`rule.forbid.buzzwords` (**extant**, ehmpathy/mechanic, **blocker**) both fire on the same sentence,
and no file says so.

| prose | `buzzwords` | `subversive-prose` |
|---|---|---|
| *"this approach is robust and scalable"* | ✅ fires | ✅ fires — 🔴 **the overlap** |
| *"we carefully considered the tradeoffs"* | ❌ no buzzword | ✅ fires |
| *"a scalable retry with exponential backoff, capped at 5"* | ✅ fires on *scalable* | ❌ disputable — go read the cap |

⇒ **neither contains the other**, so the new rule does earn its place: `buzzwords` grades **word
choice** (lexical), `subversive-prose` grades **whether the sentence asserts a checkable claim**
(semantic). row 3 is the proof — a buzzword inside a falsifiable sentence.

**the harm without the boundary:** two blockers fire on one sentence and the author cannot tell which
to repair, nor whether one repair closes both. that is a friction hazard in a rule pair whose whole
purpose is to reduce noise.

## .the shape of the fix

when `rule.forbid.subversive-prose` is authored (execution stage), it owes:

1. a `.see also` that cites `rule.forbid.buzzwords`
2. **one line that draws the boundary** — lexical vs semantic, with row 3 above as the example that
   parts them
3. 🟡 a decision on **double-grade**: does a sentence that trips both count as one blocker or two?
   my read is **one** — the repair is a single rewrite — but that is a call, not a fact

`rule.forbid.buzzwords` lives in another repo and needs no edit; the boundary belongs on the new
rule, which is the newcomer.
