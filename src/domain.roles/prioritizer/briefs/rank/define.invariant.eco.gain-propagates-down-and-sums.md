# define.invariant.eco.gain-propagates-down-and-sums

## 🔴 .SUPERSEDED — do NOT act on the rule below

gain flows UP, parts → whole, and a whole's OWN figure overrides its parts' sum. the live rule is
`define.invariant.eco.gain-sums-up-never-down`, the exact reverse of this one.

the argument that overturned it: a parent's own figure and its children's sum are two ESTIMATES
of one quantity, never two quantities — to add them counts the same dollar twice. down-propagation
put the parent's figure on the parent AND on every child, which forced this brief to forbid a
portfolio total outright (open question 2 below) — the one number a human wants most could not be
computed from the store at all.

⚠️ kept rather than deleted: its ARGUMENT outlives its conclusion. the N-birds-one-stone case it
makes is real, and is now an open fulcrum with a named candidate repair — a serve COUNT as a rank
input rather than a propagated figure.

---

## .what (historical — the superseded claim)

a surgoal feeds its gain through to every subgoal beneath it. a subgoal fed by several surgoals
sums what each feeds it — so a goal that serves three roots carries three roots' worth of gain,
and rises on the rank with no advocate.

## .kind

nature on both clauses: the direction, because a subgoal exists only as a decomposition of
something above it and holds no worth of its own; the sum, because it is arithmetic over what
actually happens — finish a subgoal that serves A and B and both advance. nurture on one clause:
the double-count guard below, a choice about how a propagated figure may be read.

## .the invariant (historical)

```
gain(goal)  =  gain.own(goal)  +  Σ  gain(parent)        over every parent edge
```

sum runs over edges, so a goal with N parents draws from all N. a root has no parent, so
`gain(root) = gain.own(root)` — the recursion terminates. the graph is acyclic by refusal at
write time.

## .why it was tried

a flat queue cannot see an N-birds-one-stone goal — three rows that each read `p1 · 3d` may serve
one root or three, and a list hides which. with propagation, the rank does that work: a
multi-parent goal rises because its gain is genuinely larger, not because someone argued for it.

## .what it left unsettled

1. **does a subgoal inherit its parent's whole gain, or a share?** if a surgoal needs five
   subgoals and each inherits the full figure, the five together read 5× the real gain —
   arguably correct for RANK (all five equally necessary, should sit equally high), plainly
   wrong for a PORTFOLIO TOTAL. unsettled.
2. **(nurture) a propagated figure is a rank signal, never an additive total** — sum a
   propagated column down the page and you double-count, since the parent's gain appears on the
   parent and again on every child. the discipline lives in the render, which must mark a
   propagated figure as propagated.
3. **does an opine propagate? no** — `sev`/`urg` are human-authored (`rule.forbid.fabricated-opines`).
   gain propagates; judgment does not.

## .the counter-argument that held in the end

a subgoal that is one of five is not worth its parent's whole gain, and to say it is inflates
every such row — a deep tree's leaves all look valuable, the same failure as a queue where every
row is p1. the defense (rank is ordinal, so the inflation is harmless in order) does not cover
question 1, and that gap is part of why the direction reversed.
