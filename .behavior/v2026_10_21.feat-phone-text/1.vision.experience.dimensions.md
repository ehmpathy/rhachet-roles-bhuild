# 1.vision.experience.dimensions — phone.text + phone.contacts

the axes the phone experience varies over. each is a small closed set; each is orthogonal to
the rest, save the one constraint named below. the product is walked in
`1.vision.experience.case=_.md`.

dimensions are named `D<n>`. the inventory names its cells by projection — `S` (send), `P`
(delivery), `G` (across groves), `C` (contacts), `A` (audit) — so a cell never shares a name with
an axis.

## .the dimensions

| # | dimension | values | why orthogonal |
|---|---|---|---|
| D1 | **actor** (who acts) | `director` · `crew.mechanic` · `crew.foreman` · `booter` (the human or director that boots a tree) · `reader` (a human or director who audits the mailbox) | a role is fixed by the tree's boot, not by what it sends or to whom |
| D2 | **act** | `text` · `reply` · `text.get` · `text.del` · `contacts.get` · `contacts.set` · `contacts.del` · `contacts.uses` · `daemon.get` · `daemon.set` · `daemon.del` · `audit` (read the mailbox dir) · `hand-edit` (write a record by hand) | an act is chosen by the actor; every act is open to more than one actor |
| D9 | **meter** (`phone.contacts.uses`) | `blocked` (default, unset) · `allowed` (a human, or a director's boot hook) | set per tree by a human; independent of role — no role reads as allowed on its own |
| D3 | **reach** (where the peer sits) | `same-tree` · `cross-tree, same grove` · `cross-grove` | where a peer lives is a fact of the peer, not of the sender's role or the act |
| D4 | **fan** (how many peers) | `one` · `group` (2+) | the count of `--to` addresses is independent of where each sits |
| D5 | **known?** (is the peer admitted) | `in contacts` · `in own tree` · `inbound` (not a contact, but an `in` record names it) · `self` · `unknown` | a contact is granted at boot, an inbound record by a peer's text; reach does not decide either |
| D12 | **address form** | `canonical` · `short` (`@:<clone>`) · `relative` (`grove=local`) · `malformed` · `out of order` | the spelling the actor typed; independent of who the address names |
| D15 | **content** (`--what`) | `single-line` · `multiline` (inline or `@stdin`) · `empty` · `oversized` | the shape of what is said; independent of to whom or how it travels |
| D13 | **text ref** (for `--re`, `--text`) | `uuid` · `alias, one match` · `alias, two matches` · `no match` · `a number` (refused) | how the actor names a text; applies only to `reply`, `text.get`, `text.del` |
| D14 | **record owner** (for `text.del`) | `own out` · `own in` · `another clone's out` (impossible: not in your mailbox) | whose record the act touches; set by who wrote it, not by who deletes |
| D6 | **peer state** at delivery | `live + idle` · `live + mid-turn` · `live + at a modal` · `live + dirty input box` · `live + screen unrecognized` · `dead` · `absent` (no such clone) | the clone's own condition, set by the clone, not by the sender |
| D7 | **daemon state** | `up` · `down` (dead, or never started) | the delivery process lifecycle; independent of the peer |
| D8 | **time** | `first` · `re-tick` (the daemon passes the same text again) · `concurrent` (two writers at once: two sets from one clone at send, or two senders to one peer at delivery) | the concurrency axis that surfaces the ordered and delivered-once invariants |
| D10 | **grove route** (how a cross-grove text moves) | `push` (the sender's grove holds the tunnel) · `pull` (the peer's grove holds it) · `relay` (a third grove holds tunnels to both) · `none` (no grove tunnels to both) | set by which grove holds which tunnel, a fact of the fleet — not of the sender, the act, or the peer's state |
| D11 | **tunnel state** | `live` · `down` | a tunnel drops and wakes on its own clock; independent of the route that would use it |
| D16 | **clone life** (of the actor or the peer) | `first life` · `revived, slug rebound` (a fresh serial under the old slug) · `revived, no slug` (a fresh serial, a new address) | a clone dies and is enrolled again on the booter's clock; independent of its role, its reach, or the act |

the daemon is not an actor. it is the condition D7 names, and its acts (a say, a pull, a failure
notice) are outcomes of a human or clone act, walked in projections 2 and 3.

## .the two constraints

**D5 `in own tree` ⟹ D3 `same-tree`, and D5 `self` ⟹ D3 `same-tree`.** a clone of one's own
tree, and oneself, cannot sit in another tree or on another grove. so `in own tree` and `self`,
each × `cross-tree` or `cross-grove`, are **impossible** (cell S20). the axes stay apart because
the converse fails: a `same-tree` peer may also be `in contacts` (S8).

## .the product

5 × 13 × 2 × 3 × 2 × 5 × 5 × 4 × 5 × 3 × 7 × 2 × 3 × 4 × 2 = 393,120,000 raw tuples (in table order: D1, D2, D9, D3, D4, D5, D12, D15, D13, D14, D6, D7, D8, D10, D11). most collapse at
once, because several axes gate only some acts:

- **D3–D8, D12, and D15 apply only to `text` and `reply`** (the two acts that move a message). D13
  applies only to `reply`, `text.get`, `text.del`; D14 only to `text.del`. `contacts.*` vary on
  D1 × D5 and D9; `daemon.*`, `audit`, and `hand-edit` on D1 alone.
- **`reply` is a `text` in the other direction** — it covers the same D3–D8 cells. its one extra
  question: does the original sender get notified? (yes — by the same delivery).
- **D6 × D7 matter only at delivery**, which is after the sender's `text` returns. so the sender's
  experience is always the same fast write; D6 × D7 shape the *outcome* the sender later reads.
- **D10 × D11 apply only where D3 = `cross-grove`.** on one grove there is no tunnel to route
  through or to lose. and `none` fails at send, so it never meets D11.

so the product is walked as five projections, each closed:

1. **send-time**: D1 × D4 × D5 × D3 × D12 × D15 × D13 × D14 × D8(`concurrent`) — does the text get accepted into the queue?
2. **delivery-time**: D6 × D7 × D8 — what becomes of an accepted text?
3. **across groves**: D10 × D11 — how does a cross-grove text move, and what if the tunnel is down?
4. **contacts**: D1 × D2(`contacts.*`) × D9 — who may read, who may grant or revoke, and who may lift the meter?
5. **audit and upkeep**: D1 × D2(`text.get`, `audit`, `hand-edit`, `daemon.*`) — who may read the record, what does it show, and who tends the daemon?

## .the hidden dimensions the walk surfaced

- **tree, not repo.** the D3 × D5 cells showed that a repo name alone does not name a peer: one
  repo can hold several worktrees, each its own tree with its own `@:mechanic`. the address names
  the tree, which already pins the repo and the branch (F2)
- **grove route.** the cross-grove cells showed a tunnel opens one way, so a reply from a remote
  grove has no route back unless the tunnel holder pulls it. that is D10, and it decides the
  delivery mechanism, not merely its speed
- **a number is not a name.** the D3 × `reply` cells showed a per-mailbox count gives one text a
  different number in each mailbox, so a reply hint that names a number names the wrong text, and
  a mistyped number names a valid neighbor. a text's one name is its uuid, shared by its `out` and
  `in` copies; a number is refused (F9). that is D13
- **clone, not tree.** the D1 × D2 cells showed two clones in one tree must not read, reply to, or
  withdraw each other's texts. so the mailbox and the contacts are per clone, keyed by its serial
  (F12); the daemon and the meter stay per tree. that is why D14 `another clone's out` is
  impossible
- **address form, text ref, record owner.** first walked as extra values of D5 `known?`. each is
  its own fact — the spelling typed, the way a text is named, whose record it is — so each is its
  own axis (D12, D13, D14)
- **clone life.** the i007 peer review walked the self side of a revive, which P6 had walked only
  from the peer's side. a revive mints a fresh serial under the old slug, and the phone keys its
  state by seat (F24, ruled), so the revived clone keeps the seat's mailbox and contacts (case=11,
  C9); a clone with no slug has no seat to keep (C10). that is D16; it varies the contacts and send
  projections, and P6 on the peer side
- **content.** the peer review asked what becomes of a multiline, empty, or oversized `--what`.
  the shape of what is said is a fact of its own (D15); a multiline text that a newline submits
  early would split one message into two
