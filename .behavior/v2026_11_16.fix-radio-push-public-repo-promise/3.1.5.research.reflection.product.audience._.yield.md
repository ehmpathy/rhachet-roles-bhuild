# reflection.audience: who the public-repo promise is for

## who is the audience?

- **primary — a clone that pushes follow-ups across repos.** a mechanic or supervisor clone, mid-route
  in a private repo, that files a task into another repo with `rhx radio.task.push`. its notes hold
  private context: the source repo, the route path, pr numbers, service and table names. it writes the
  title and body from those notes, often via `--description @stdin`, and runs unattended
- **secondary — the commander and the org whose detail could leak.** the human who owns the private
  repos and ranked this p0. they never see the push; they see the leak, after
- **secondary — the human who pushes by hand** with `--auth as-human`, into a public repo of the
  ehmpathy org (rhachet, the role packages)
- **tertiary — the maintainers and readers of the public repos.** they receive the issues; a scrubbed
  issue is one they can act on without a guess at private context
- **tertiary — the drain operator.** a human or clone that runs `rhx radio.task.queue drain` days
  later, over records it did not write

## why do they care?

- the clone: it has no way today to know a target is public, and no moment to re-read its text. the
  nudge gives it both, once, with the exact next command — no doc to read, no new skill to learn
- the commander: a public issue cannot be taken back (claims yield, L1). edit history and public
  archives keep the first text. the gate is the only control at the right moment
- the human pusher: the same protection, with no ceremony on private repos
- the drain operator: a held public record names why it waits and how to land it or drop it, so no
  record is lost and none is sent unchecked

## how much do they care?

| audience | priority | urgency | stakes |
|---|---|---|---|
| primary: the clone that pushes | high — every cross-repo push passes this gate | high — clones push many times a day | a public leak it cannot undo; or, if the gate is noisy, friction on every push |
| secondary: the commander / org | highest — ranked p0 | high — due at the 2026-11-17 window | private repo, customer, revenue detail public, permanently |
| secondary: the human pusher | medium | medium | same leak, rarer |
| tertiary: public repo readers | low | low | an issue with private references they cannot follow |
| tertiary: the drain operator | medium | low | a record held with no clear fix, or sent unchecked |

## what do they care about?

- **zero cost on private repos.** the clone judges the change by whether its common path changed:
  private and internal pushes must send as today, no nudge, no flag. a nudge on a private repo would
  train the reflex the claims yield warns of (L5)
- **a nudge that says exactly what to check and what to run.** three kinds of detail to look for, the
  repo marked `(public)`, the exact command with `--promise` appended
- **no lost task.** a nudged push leaves no stale twin; a held drain record waits with its fix
- **fail closed, but say why.** an unreadable visibility reads `(visibility unreadable — treated as
  public)`, so a token fault is not mistaken for a public repo
- tradeoffs accepted: one extra gh call per gated send; one extra pass for public text
- tradeoffs rejected: a scanner that blocks on false positives; a prompt that waits on a tty; a vouch
  remembered across pushes
- delight: the gate is invisible on private repos and one re-run on public ones. disappointment: a
  nudge on a status-only update, or on a private repo, or a drain that halts the whole line for one
  public record

## how will they experience it?

touchpoints, in order:
1. `radio.task.push … --into <public repo>` → exit 2, the 🦫 nudge (stdout)
2. a re-read of the title and body, a scrub if needed
3. the re-run of the printed command, `--promise task-to-public-repo-is-anonymized` → `🦫 delivered`
4. later, if held while radio was blocked: `rhx radio.task.queue get` names the record, its reason,
   its fix; `rhx radio.task.queue drain --promise …` sends it
5. `--help` on push and queue names the flag

friction points:
- the printed command holds `@stdin` for a piped body; the clone must re-pipe the same body. the nudge
  reprints `@stdin` as typed, so the re-run is a copy plus the pipe
- a clone that habituates may re-run without a re-read. mitigation: rarity (private repos see no
  nudge), the checklist names concrete kinds of detail, the vouch is per push
- a drain `--promise` vouches every held public record at once (F3); the fix lines point at `queue get`
  first, so the operator reads the titles before the vouch

smooth: a private push is unchanged; a public push costs one read of the checklist and one re-run.
