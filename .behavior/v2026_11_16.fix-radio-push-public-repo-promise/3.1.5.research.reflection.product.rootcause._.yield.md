# reflection.rootcause: why a push to a public repo needs a promise

## what is the symptom?

- **absent:** `radio.task.push` sends a task's title and body into any repo it is pointed at, public
  or private, with no check of who will read it
- **manifests as:** a public github issue whose text names a private repo, a route path, a pr
  number, an internal service, or business detail. its first text stays in edit history and public
  archives (claims yield, L1)
- **occurs when:** a clone in a private repo files a follow-up into a public one (an ehmpathy role
  package, rhachet) — by push, by the backlog flush of a later push, or by a queue drain

## 5 whys tree

### branch 1: the text holds private detail

1. why does private detail reach a public issue? → the clone writes the task from its own notes, and
   its notes hold the context of the private repo it works in
2. why does it not scrub them? → at the moment of the push, it does not know the target is public,
   and naught asks it to re-read
3. why does it not know? → the radio treats every target the same; visibility is never read
4. why is visibility never read? → the radio was built for the crew's own repos, where every target
   was private; public targets arrived later, with the role packages
5. why does that matter now? → **the send is the one irreversible act, and the radio holds no model
   of the target's audience at that act** — potential root cause

### branch 2: the clone should know better

1. why not teach clones to scrub? → a brief can say so, but a brief is read at boot, far from the push
2. why is a brief not enough? → a rule recalled from memory at the moment of an act is the weakest
   control (claims yield, L5: warnings fade even when seen at the moment)
3. why not trust the clone's judgment? → the clone cannot tell which of its own words are private:
   the org name it works in every day reads as ordinary to it
4. why can it not tell? → privacy is a property of the audience, not of the words
5. why does that point back? → **the check belongs where the audience is known: at the send** —
   converges with branch 1

### branch 3: a content scanner (ruled out)

1. why not scan the text for private detail? → org names, route paths, and pr numbers match no
   pattern a scanner knows (claims yield, L8; access yield, L10)
2. → **ruled out** as the root fix; a scanner could assist later, never replace the author's check

## root cause summary

| layer | issue | is root cause? |
|---|---|---|
| the clone's notes | they hold private context | no — the notes are correct for the private repo |
| the clone's judgment | it cannot see which words are private | no — privacy is a property of the audience |
| the push cli | no flag, no nudge | no — a symptom; a cli-only gate misses drain and flush |
| the per-record send | no read of the target's audience before an irreversible act | **yes** |

## recommendation

- **address root cause:** give the per-record send (`setRadioTaskRecordUpstream`, the one path push,
  backlog flush, and drain share) a read of the target's visibility, and hold text bound for a public
  or unreadable repo until its author vouches it is anonymized. the gate lives where the audience is
  known and where the act is irreversible; every path inherits it
- the promise itself treats a symptom of the deeper fact that only the author can judge its text. that
  part is irreducible: no tool can know which words are private. the gate's job is to make the author
  look, at the right moment, and say so
- follow-ups the root fix does not cover, each to be caught as a dream at execution and symlinked
  under this route's `dreams/`:
  - a promise bound to the target repo (`--promise <owner/name>`-style), per the confirmation-flag
    sources (access yield, L9) — harder to habituate
  - one shared stub `gh` factory for the six test copies (flagged yield, topic 3)
  - a private repo that later turns public publishes its history (claims yield, L1 [KHUE]) — out of
    the radio's reach
