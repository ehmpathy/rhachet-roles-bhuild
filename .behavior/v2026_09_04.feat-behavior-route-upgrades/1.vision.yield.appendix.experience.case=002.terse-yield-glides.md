# case=002 — a terse yield glides, and the human reads it in one pass

> **who** — a driver clone at `1.vision`, and the wisher who reads what it emits
> **came for** — driver: to pass the gate without a trim cycle. wisher: to learn what the stone decided, in one read.
>
> cell — `actor=driver · lens=prose · feel=happy · care=alterpath`
> plus `actor=wisher · lens=prose · feel=happy · care=alterpath` at `[t2]` — the wisher's read
>
> 🟡 **both cells are alterpaths, and both are demoed.** the prose gate is new, so each fallback is
> the world before it: the driver's is a verbose yield (a cost per round, never a failure); the
> wisher's is to diff the dated layers by hand. they are demoed anyway because this walk is the
> whole point of the lens, and because the driver's walk **ends** at the wisher's read — a terse
> yield with no reader who benefits would be terseness for its own sake.

## the narrative

**as the driver.** before i write the yield, i answer the self review `has-terse-prose`. it does not
grade my draft — it changes what i draft. it asks me to lead with the defect as a table, to state
each decision once, to put every caveat at the instruction it modifies, and to cite inline. i write
199 lines instead of 615, on the first pass.

the peer prose lane runs and returns `0 blockers`. i never enter a trim cycle, because the
self review is upstream of the peer lane and the peer lane only confirms.

**as the wisher.** i open `1.vision.yield.md` to decide whether to approve. the first item i meet is
a table, not a preamble. i read the section titles alone and leave with the answers, because each
title names a conclusion rather than a topic. the citations sit inside the sentences that make the
claims, so i never hold a pointer to an evidence appendix.

i do not diff dated layers to find which claims still hold, because there are no dated layers. the
yield states what is.

## the demo

```gherkin
given a 1.vision guard whose self review ladder includes has-terse-prose
  and the driver has not yet drafted 1.vision.yield.md

[t0] the driver answers the self review before it drafts
  when it applies the howto: lead with the defect as a table, declare once,
       caveat inline, cite inline, name each section title by its answer
  then the emitted 1.vision.yield.md carries every owed claim, citation, and demo
   and no round is spent to remove what was never written

[t1] the peer prose lane runs
  when the lane greps each decision term across the yield
  then no term appears in more than 3 places
   and no section title matches /^#{2,3} .*\bi\d{3}\b/
   and the lane returns 0 blockers
   and the driver enters no trim cycle

[t2] the wisher reads the yield to approve the stone
  when the wisher reads only the section titles
  then the wisher leaves with the decisions
   and the wisher performs no diff across dated layers to find the current truth
```

## what this proves

- **the self review is the higher-leverage half.** it changes what gets written; the peer lane only
  trims what was. `[t0]` is where the 3x reduction happens, and `[t1]` merely confirms it.
- the deterministic pre-check (`/^#{2,3} .*\bi\d{3}\b/`) costs no brain call, so the cheap half of
  the gate runs first.
- terse is not thin. the 615→199 cut drops restatement only, and `[t0]`'s `then` clauses name what
  survives. why a reader is what makes the cut worth its cost is stated in the header, above.
