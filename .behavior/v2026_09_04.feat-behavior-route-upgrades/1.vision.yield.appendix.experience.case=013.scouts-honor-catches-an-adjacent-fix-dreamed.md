# case=013 — the scouts-honor lane catches an adjacent fix that was dreamed

> **who** — a driver of a bhuild behavior route, mid-execution
> **came for** — to close the stone and move on to the next
>
> cell — `actor=driver · lens=scouts-honor · feel=sharp · care=alterpath`

## the narrative

**as the driver.** i am deep in execution. i open a readme to cite one line, and i notice it shouts
its headers in caps and stacks three emoji on a bullet — noise the repo's own prose rules forbid.
the fix is four `sedreplace` edits in the file already under my cursor. it touches no behavior, it
ripples into no other file, it is adjacent to the exact work i came for.

i defer it. i write a dream — `$route/dreams/v2026_09_14.cut-emphasis-noise-in-readme.md` — symlink
it into `.dream/`, and tell myself i will sweep it later.

🔴 **that deferral is the defect.** the fix was SAFE (no behavior beyond the readme), CLEAN (lands in
the diff already open, zero ripple), and ADJACENT (i was in the file). `rule.always.fix-forward-under-scouts-honor`
is explicit: a fix that passes both questions is done NOW. a dream is the second-best outcome, earned
only by a large / dirty fix that ripples — and this one does neither.

**the peer lane reads the dream and flags it.** at the vision + execution guards, `scouts-honor-fix-forward`
globs `$route/dreams/*.md`, reads my dream against the fix-forward rules, and blocks: this dream
describes a small in-scope fix, so it was a fix, never a follow-up.

## the demo

```gherkin
given a driver mid-execution who spots a safe + clean + adjacent readme fix
  and the fix is four sedreplace edits in a file already open in the diff
  and the scouts-honor-fix-forward peer lane on the vision + execution guards

[t0] the driver defers the fix to a dream instead of doing it now
  when they write $route/dreams/v2026_09_14.cut-emphasis-noise-in-readme.md
   and symlink it into .dream/
  then the fix that cost seconds now costs a full re-derivation later
   and the deferral is silent — the dream reads like diligence, not neglect

[t1] the peer lane grades the dream against the fix-forward rules
  when the driver arrives the stone and the scouts-honor lane runs
  then it reads $route/dreams/*.md and finds a dream describing a small in-scope fix
   and it blocks: a safe + clean + adjacent fix is done now, never dreamed
   and it names the fix — do the sedreplace edits in-scope, delete the dream

[t2] the driver does the fix in-scope and the lane clears
  when they apply the four edits to the readme in the open diff
   and remove the dream + its symlink
  then the dreams/ dir holds only large / dirty deferrals
   and the scouts-honor lane renders a clean verdict
```

## the fallback is why this cell is alterpath, not critipath

🟡 **the caught dream is recoverable.** the deferred fix survives in `$route/dreams/` with its full
record — the cue that fired, the shape of the fix, the reason it was deferred. a later round can pick
it up and do it. so a single missed deferral does not silently lose the work: it costs a full
re-derivation of context the driver already held, but the fix itself is not gone.

⇒ that recoverability is the whole difference from a critipath. contrast `case=010` (a re-init keeps
the old guard and reports no gap — a silent loss) or `case=011` (a dispatch with no bound route — no
lever the driver holds). here the fallback is a queue entry, not a void.

**so why demo an alterpath?** because the deferral is the whole point of the lens. `rule.require.experience-coverage`
lets an alterpath stay itemized — but this one is demoed because it is the critical *behavior* the
2026-09-14 steer added the lane to catch. the recoverability makes the cell alterpath; the intent
makes it worth a `case=N`.

## the over-fire twin, and its fail-safe

`lane × scouts-honor × sharp` is the mirror: the lane flags a legitimate large / dirty dream as a
small fix. the fail-safe is a `.taken` refute — the driver names the ripple
(*"this rename touches 40 callers across 6 files"*), and the lane drops the point next round. a dream
that is genuinely a big-dirty deferral is exactly what a dream is FOR (`rule.always.catch-dreams-for-followups`),
so the lane must yield to a driver who can name the ripple.

| the dream | the lane | why |
|-----------|----------|-----|
| a small safe + clean + adjacent fix | **blocker** | it was a fix; the driver holds the lever to do it now |
| a large / dirty fix that ripples | **clean** | the deferral is correct; a dream is what it is for |

⇒ the split satisfies `rule.forbid.overzealous-blockers`: the lane blocks only where a fix can be
named and the driver can run it. where the deferral is legitimate, the lane reports no point.

## what this proves

- the scouts-honor discipline is a driver-owned close, checked at the stage the fix is spotted —
  never once at the terminal council, because by the terminal stone the context that made the fix
  cheap is gone (`define.invariant.scouts-honor.fix-forward`).
- 🟡 **a deferral that reads like diligence is the dangerous shape.** a dream *looks* like a record of
  responsible work — which is exactly why the lane is needed: nobody flags their own dream, because
  writing it down felt like the responsible move.
- the lane's subject `$route/dreams/*` is route-local, so — unlike the dispatch lint of `case=012` —
  it can only ever block the work that produced the dream. no blast radius beyond the route under drive.
