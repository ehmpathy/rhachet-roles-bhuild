## title

fix(review): close the flag landmine — a misused flag must fail loud, never decide quietly

## body

## the ask

`parseReviewArgs` (`src/contract/cli/review.ts`) handles three classes of caller error **quietly**.
each one hands the caller a lane that runs, returns a confident verdict, and graded a corpus nobody
asked for.

> **a review lane pointed at the wrong corpus does not fall silent. it reasons correctly from what
> it was shown.**

⇒ that is what makes this a landmine rather than a bug: **the output is well-formed in every case.**
no surface a driver reads distinguishes it from a real verdict.

### the three, and each is measured

| # | the misuse | what happens now | who carries the evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | an **unrecognized** flag — `--paths-without` | parsed and discarded | bhrain#446 §1 — cited, not re-derived |
| 2 | a **doubled single-value** flag — `--paths-with X --paths-with Y` | last-wins; the first value vanishes | **this issue** — bhuild#366, 3 lanes over 4 rounds |
| 3 | `--paths-with` with **no explicit `--diffs`** | the diff bound is **replaced** rather than narrowed | bhrain#446 §2, and bhrain `.dream/v2026_09_05.*` |

🟡 **#2 is the one worth the closest look, because the caller's form is REASONABLE.** repetition is
how `--refs` and `--optional` already behave in this same parser
(`review.ts:144-149` accumulates those two, and only those two). so a caller who repeats
`--paths-with` has generalized correctly from the tool's own extant behavior — and is silently wrong.

### the cost, by polarity

from bhuild#366's measurement across four rounds on the three doubled lanes:

| polarity | count | what it cost |
|---|---|---|
| a false **blocker** — coverage denied a test that exists on disk | 2 | a round, reviewer budget, and a driver directed to author a test already written |
| a false clean **`0/0`** | 1 | a gap indistinguishable from a real pass |
| a **true** catch, by coincidence of the narrowed set | 1 | — |

🔴 **the false-blocker polarity is the dearer, because it actively directs work.** a driver who
complies rather than checks lands a duplicate test and calls it a repair.

## the shape of the fix

### 1. declare each flag's arity, then enforce it

```
accumulate:   --rules · --refs · --optional
single:       --paths-with · --paths-wout · --diffs · --output · --mode · --join · --goal
```

- an **unrecognized** flag → exit nonzero, name the flag, list the accepted set
- a **doubled single-value** flag → exit nonzero, name the flag and both values

🟡 **do NOT repair a doubled single-value flag by union.** it looks generous and it is a second
guess at intent — the caller may have meant either. `rule.require.failfast`: reject at the boundary.

### 2. echo the computed scope where the caller sees it

the recorded scope in the log (`paths: **/*.snap`) is what exposed defect 2 — but only to someone
who went and looked. **print the computed target-set summary on the lane's own stdout**, so a scope
collapse is visible in the round it happens rather than in a later audit.

### 3. pair with bhrain#446

bhrain#446 also carries the structural default. **this one adds defect 2 and the arity contract that
closes all three at once.** they may ship together; the arity declaration is the single mechanism
under both.

## the acceptance

- an unrecognized flag exits nonzero and names it — no value is ever dropped
- `--paths-with X --paths-with Y` exits nonzero and names both values
- `--refs A --refs B` still accumulates, unchanged
- every lane's stdout carries a one-line computed-scope summary
- a fixture test per class, so a future parser refactor cannot silently restore last-wins

## why an alias or a union is the wrong repair

both repair **the instance** and leave **the class** open. an alias for `--paths-without` fixes one
typo and the next one is still silent; a union for doubled `--paths-with` fixes this shape and the
next single-value flag someone doubles is still handled quietly.

⇒ **the defect is not any one flag. it is that the parser guesses rather than refuses.**
