## title

teach the telepath to sort subversive prose into its two shapes — shallow vs squishy

## body

`rule.forbid.subversive-prose` is authored in `rhachet-roles-bhuild` on the
`feat-behavior-route-upgrades` route. its test is:

> **what observation would settle this sentence?**

🔴 **not** *"could a reader disagree with it?"* — that is a test of **taste**, and a metaphor passes
it. you may disagree with *"it took the cheaper of two doors"*, and no observation on earth settles
the disagreement.

### the two shapes

| shape | reads as | what is absent | example |
|---|---|---|---|
| **shallow** | *insubstantial* | no substance beneath it — the sentence asserts none | *"we carefully considered the tradeoffs"* |
| **squishy** | *unjustifiable* | no justification reaches it — it conforms to whatever the reader wants | *"robust and scalable"* · *"the cheaper of two doors"* · *"✅ F7 free"* |

🟡 `unjustifiable` carries a second sense in plain english — *morally indefensible* — so **`squishy`
is the canonical term** and `unjustifiable` is its gloss, never a synonym to use in a contract.

### why one question is not enough

the same claim, written three ways:

| # | prose | is there a claim? | what observation settles it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | *"the review flow has some issues worth a look"* | ❌ | — |
| 2 | *"it took the cheaper of two doors that both looked legitimate"* | ✅ | 🔴 **none** |
| 3 | *"`runStoneGuardReviews` never calls `getStone…Unforgiven`, so the check runs at passage"* | ✅ | ✅ open the file |

⇒ a test that asks **"is there a claim?"** answers yes on rows 2 **and** 3. it catches the cheapest
instance and passes the expensive one.

🔴 **squishy is the dangerous shape.** shallow prose reads as filler and a reviewer cuts it on
sight. squishy prose reads as **insight** — the author writes it and feels lucid, the reader nods,
and the mechanism it stood in front of is never stated. it takes the slot a settleable claim was owed.

### what the telepath owes

1. **sort, do not merely flag.** report which shape fired. the repairs differ: shallow prose is
   **cut**; squishy prose is **replaced** by the mechanism it stood in front of
2. **run the substitution sub-test on squishy prose** — substitute the sentence for the mechanism it
   describes. if the reader can no longer act on it, the sentence displaced rather than explained
3. **hold the false-positive bound.** the defect is **ambiguity**, never a rhetorical register. a
   metaphor that *accompanies* a stated mechanism is fine; one that *replaces* it is not
4. **terse is not thin.** a settleable claim stays, however long it runs

### the lesson worth a keep

> **a test that answers YES on both the good and the bad case is not a test — it is a filter one
> notch too coarse.**

before any rule ships: write the defect three ways — absent, plausible-but-wrong, and correct — and
confirm the test separates all three. a test that separates only the first from the rest catches the
cheapest instance and passes the expensive one.

⇒ this is `rule.require.enumerate-before-you-name` applied to a **rule's scope** rather than a word,
and that is the sharper case: a wrong word costs a rename; a wrong scope ships a gate that passes
the defect it was built for.
