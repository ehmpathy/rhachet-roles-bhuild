## .settled

### 1. the four verdicts DESCRIBE; this act PRESCRIBES

`demoed` · `itemized` · `forbidden` · `impossible` all classify a cell the walk **found**. not one
asks whether the design could have deleted it.

⇒ so the extant discipline grades **coverage**, and coverage is satisfied by a space of any size —
a 200-cell space with 200 verdicts scores exactly as well as a 40-cell one, and costs five times as
much to hold, test, and support.

### 2. the grain is a SUBSET — a row, a column, a plane

a per-cell question answers *"this cell is fine"* every time, because each cell alone is defensible.
the prune only becomes visible at the subset: hold a whole plane and ask what it **collectively**
earns.

### 3. the three levers are a ladder, and the wisher named them in cost order

| lever | the subset goes from |
|---|---|
| **guard** — the attempt cannot be made | N → **impossible** |
| **redirect** — the attempt funnels into an extant path | N → **0** new |
| **forbid** — the attempt is refused, loud | N → **1** sharp rejection |

⇒ reach for the highest rung the domain permits, and name which rung you took — it fixes what the
design now owes.

## .the two additions bhuild made, and why

| addition | why |
|---|---|
| **merge before you prune** | a subset that reads prunable is sometimes a subset the *model* invented. a merge is free; a prune is a design decision. to prune first is to ship a forbid against a combination that was never real |
| **a prune states its COST** | a prune is a capability removed. one with no stated cost reads as a free simplification, and no such prune exists — the cost column is the half a reviewer needs and the half an author omits |

`lane × sweep` cells were already guard-lever prunes recorded as `impossible` — a description with
