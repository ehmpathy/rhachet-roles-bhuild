# vision: every vision digs to its root purpose

## .the root purpose

- why: a vision must say why it is wanted, not only what it builds
  - why: a reviewer reads the motive before the shape — without it, the reviewer grades a shape against no aim, and approves whatever looks tidy
    - why: work that serves no stated motive drifts; the builder ships what was easy to build, and the wisher receives an artifact they did not want
      - why: the route exists to turn a wish into what the wisher actually needs; a motive kept implicit is the single cheapest place for that to fail

## .the outcome world

### before

a clone runs `init.behavior`, reads the wish, and writes `1.vision.yield.md`. the yield holds an outcome world, experiences, an evaluation — and no statement of *why* the work matters. the peer lanes on `1.vision` grade the experience space (`dimensional-decomposition`, `experience-coverage`); none asks for a motive. a vision that never names its purpose passes.

the reviewer opens the yield and meets the shape first. the motive, if any, lives in the wish's `.why` — one line, often a mechanism (*"so the guard runs X"*).

### after

the `1.vision.stone` scaffold carries a `## .the root purpose` section: three nested `- why:` bullets, each a motive under the one above. the clone fills it before all else, since the scaffold puts it first.

two new peer lanes run on `1.vision`, in both guard variants:

| lane | how | refuses |
|---|---|---|
| `root-purpose-depth` | deterministic — `rhx review.vision.root-purpose` parses the yield | section absent · under 3 layers · a layer left as scaffold text |
| `root-purpose-motive` | brain — `rhx review` against `rule.require.vision-root-purpose` | a layer, or the root, that names a mechanism rather than a motive. raises a nitpick (never a block) on a shape that never answers to its root |

the reviewer opens the yield and reads the motive in its first ten lines.

### the aha

the third `why` is where the mechanism runs out. *"add a peer lane"* → *"so the vision is checked"* → *"so the work serves the wisher, not the builder's ease"*. the clone that writes the third line discovers what the work is for — and the rest of the vision is written against it.

## .the contract

### the section, in the stone scaffold

```md
## .the root purpose

why is this vision wanted at all? dig at least 3 layers: each a motive (why), never a mechanism (how).

- why: <the surface motive — why the wisher asked>
  - why: <the motive beneath it>
    - why: <the root motive — the one that no longer shifts>
```

- a **layer** = one `- why:` bullet. the **depth** = the longest chain of `- why:` bullets, each nested one level under the last
- the **root** = the deepest layer of that chain
- more than three layers is welcome; fewer is refused

### the deterministic lane

```sh
rhx review.vision.root-purpose --vision .behavior/v2026_11_08.sandpine-lesson-reminders/1.vision.yield.md
```

```
🦫 root purpose
   ├─ vision: .behavior/v2026_11_08.sandpine-lesson-reminders/1.vision.yield.md
   ├─ section: present
   ├─ layers: 2 of 3
   ├─ blockers
   │  └─ [blocker][better] the root purpose is 2 layers deep; it owes at least 3
   │     └─ fix: ask why of the deepest layer, and nest the answer as a `- why:` under it
   └─ summary
      ├─ 1 blocker
      └─ 0 nitpicks
```

it satisfies `contract.reviewer-output`: two numeric lines, every time. exit 0 on a verdict, blockers included — the guard reads the counts, as it does for `rhx review`, which also exits 0 on a completed review (`rhachet-roles-bhrain/dist/contract/cli/review.js:624`). exit 2 only when `--vision` is absent or unreadable, which bhrain classes as a constraint (`asExitCodeClass.js`).

### the brain lane

`rhx review --rules '.agent/repo=bhuild/role=behaver/briefs/practices/behavior.vision/rule.require.vision-root-purpose.md' --paths-with '$route/1.vision.yield.md' …` — the rule names two judgments the depth lane cannot make: (1) each layer, and the root most of all, names a motive, never a mechanism; (2) the vision's shape — outcome world, contract, evaluation — answers to the root. the depth lane owns the count, so this lane grades only *what* the layers name and *whether the vision serves it*. its globs reach the yield alone; the experience files are the experience lanes' concern.

## .mental model

- **to a friend:** *"every vision now has to answer 'why' three times before it's allowed to say 'how'."*
- **analogy:** the five whys of a root-cause review, run forward — toward the motive, not the defect
- **their words vs ours:** they say *"the point of it"*; we say *root purpose*. they say *"how it works"*; we say *mechanism*

## .evaluation

| goal (from the wish) | met by |
|---|---|
| a reviewer reads the motive before the shape | the section sits first in the scaffold |
| the builder knows why it did the work | the third layer forces it to name the motive |
| the vision is checked to address its purpose | two peer lanes refuse an absent, shallow, or mechanism-rooted purpose — and the motive lane flags, as a nitpick, a shape that ignores its root (case=6) |
| a clamp red on 0 or 2 layers, green on 3 | the depth lane is deterministic, so its test is too |

pros: cheap to write (three lines), deterministic where it can be, brain-judged only where judgment is required.

cons: two lanes where the wish names one reviewer (see fulcrum F1). the motive lane adds one brain round per vision iteration.

edgecases, and the pit of success:
- **scaffold left verbatim** — the depth lane reads a layer whose whole text is one `<…>` placeholder as unfilled, and blocks; a clone cannot pass by an untouched template. a motive that merely quotes `<Component>` still counts
- **flat bullets** (three `- why:` at one indent) — depth 1, not 3; the fix line says *nest it*
- **extant routes** — `init.behavior` findserts guards, so a route inited before this change keeps its old guard and gets no lane. `rhx route.guard.upgrade --stone 1.vision` adopts it (itemized, not demoed). it overwrites the whole guard from its template, so a route with hand edits to its vision guard previews the diff in plan mode first

## .what is awkward?

- **two lanes for one rule.** the split is driven by testability, not by the domain. a reader may ask why `root-purpose` is graded twice
- **motive vs mechanism is a judgment.** the brain lane can mis-grade a terse motive as a mechanism. the rule's examples are the only calibration
- **extant routes are silent.** the change reaches new routes only; an in-flight route needs an explicit `route.guard.upgrade`
- **the dot in the header.** `## .the root purpose` sits beside `## the outcome world`; one of the two conventions looks wrong
