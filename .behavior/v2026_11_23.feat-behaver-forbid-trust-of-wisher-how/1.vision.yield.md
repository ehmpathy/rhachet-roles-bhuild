# vision: the behaver trusts a wisher's why and rough contract, never the how

## .what this builds

one forbid rule in the behaver role, wired into the four places a vision or criteria writer acts:

| artifact | what it does |
|---|---|
| `behavior.wish/rule.forbid.trust-of-wisher-how.md` | the cue: trust levels, the strike test, enforcement (`say`) |
| `…trust-of-wisher-how.reason.md` | the argument: why a wisher's how misleads, the faster-horse ground (credited to ford, a published source), the bounds (`ref`) |
| `…trust-of-wisher-how.example=rhachet-760.md` | the worked case, dated and sourced, in the brief's own words; the pr is linked, its review never quoted (`ref`) |
| `boot.yml` | says the rule, refs the reason + example, raises `budget.tokens` |
| `1.vision.stone` · `2.1.criteria.blackbox.stone` | one line, right after the wish is named, that points the writer at the rule. the criteria stone's stale `1.vision.md` ref becomes `1.vision.yield.md` on the way through |
| `1.vision.guard.{light,heavy}` · `2.1.criteria.blackbox.guard.heavy` | self-review `has-restated-the-wisher-how` |
| `1.vision.guard.{light,heavy}` | peer lane `wisher-how`: loads the rule, counts blockers; the extant `reviewed? --allow-blockers 0` judge holds the stone on one |
| `3.3.1.blueprint.product.guard.{light,heavy}` | the same lane, graded over the criteria and blueprint yields, so a criteria slip on a light route (no criteria guard) is blocked before any code (F7) |

## .why it works

the defect shows up in the yield, but the decision behind it was made earlier: the writer read a how in the wish as part of the
spec. so the rule must reach the writer **before** the first word, and again **at review**:

- **boot** — the cue sits in every behaver session; a vision writer meets it before the wish
- **stone** — the vision and criteria stones name the rule at the moment of authorship
- **guard, self** — a self-review makes the writer apply the strike test to the yield before it passes
- **guard, peer** — on vision and on blueprint, a peer lane grades the yields against the rule. today no guard loads any `behavior.wish/` rule, so without these lanes the rule's blockers would be graded by no one (F5, F7)

each layer is cheap. together they cover the two stages where #760 slipped (vision, requirements).

## .the rule, in one table

| what the wish carries | trust | the writer does |
|---|---|---|
| the why (purpose, benefit, the pain) | **full** | adopt it |
| the contract asked for — verbs, rough shape | **rough** | keep the verbs; challenge each input, default, scope |
| a how — poc, prior command, parity target, named mechanism, field/flag list, storage layout, a done-when phrased by a prior tool | **none, as spec** | read it as evidence; restate it in domain terms; keep what holds, drop what doesn't, record which |
| a how the wisher ties to a domain why ("secrets never leave the keyrack") | **as a constraint** | keep it, stated with its why, never with the tool's name |

the test: **strike the prior tool or poc from the repo. does each requirement still read, and still look right?** no → it
copied a how.

enforcement, as #715 proposes; the role's taxonomy (`forbid` → blocker) agrees:

- a requirement whose sense depends on a prior tool, poc, or wisher-named implementation = **blocker**
- a vision or blueprint that adopts a poc's design with no skeptic's pass recorded = **blocker** (#715 names the vision; the blueprint joins it, F4)
- a code comment or doc that cites a prior tool as a choice's reason = **nitpick**

## .the outcome world

**before.** a wish says *"`get` should show what `brains.auth.usage` shows; see the swap command for `use`."* the driver
writes case=27 (`get` shows the windows `brains.auth.usage` shows) and case=28 (three scopes, as the poc had them); per
#715, eight source comments cite the poc. the commander rejects all of it: the work existed to replace that tool.

**after.** the same wish lands on a driver who booted the cue. at vision, the yield holds a `## .the wisher's how` table:

| how in the wish | restated in domain terms | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| parity with `brains.auth.usage` | per account: each usage window, its used share, its reset | keep the facts; drop the tool as the yardstick |
| the swap command's three scopes | which seats must change account, and who asks | challenge: is scope an explicit input? → open question for the wisher |

the guard's self-review strikes the swap command and re-reads each requirement. case=27 now reads *"per account, each
window's used share and reset"* — true with or without the old tool.

**the aha.** a requirement that names a prior tool can never outlive that tool. the strike test exposes it in one read.

## .what the reader sees — rendered

the cue a behaver session boots with (`say`, the rule file):

```md
# rule.forbid.trust-of-wisher-how

> trust a wisher's **why**. trust the **contract** they ask for roughly — its verbs and rough shape. never trust their **how**.

a how in a wish is evidence to learn from, never a spec: a poc or prior command · a parity target set by a prior tool · a named mechanism, field list, flag set, or storage layout · a done-when phrased by a prior tool.

at vision and criteria: restate each how in the domain's own terms, then challenge it. keep what holds, drop what doesn't, and record which.

test: **strike the prior tool or poc from the repo. does each requirement still read, and still look right?** no → it copied a how.

worked case: ehmpathy/rhachet#760 defined `get` as "shows what `brains.auth.usage` shows"; strike that tool and the requirement says naught (→ `…example=rhachet-760.md`).

a how the wisher ties to a domain why is a constraint: keep it, stated with its why.

the inverse slip: a verb the wisher asked for, dropped as "a how". the verbs stand; only the inputs, defaults, and scope behind them are open.

blocker: a requirement whose sense depends on a prior tool, poc, or wisher-named implementation · a vision or blueprint that adopts a poc's design with no skeptic's pass recorded.
nitpick: a comment or doc that cites a prior tool as a choice's reason.
```

## .mental model

- **to a friend:** *"the wisher is the customer. listen to what hurts and what they want to press. their sketch of the
  machine is a clue, not a blueprint."*
- **the analogy:** ford's faster horse. the wisher who asks for a faster horse is right about speed and wrong about horses.
- **their words vs ours:** they say *"do what X does"*; we say *"a parity target defined by an implementation"*. they say
  *"done when it matches X"*; we say *"a done-when phrased by a prior tool"*.

## .evaluation

| goal (wish `.done means`) | met by |
|---|---|
| rule in the behaver role, booted where vision and criteria writers act | `say` cue in `boot.yml` + stone pointers + guard self-reviews |
| the issue's test and worked case | both in the said cue (the case as one line); the full case in `example=rhachet-760` at ref |
| enforcement levels as proposed, or the reason recorded | as proposed; taxonomy agrees (F4) |
| pr green, on the review list, closes #715 | execution + verification stones; the pr body carries `closes ehmpathy/rhachet-roles-bhuild#715` |

**pros**
- the rule acts before the yield exists, so it prevents the slip, not only flags it after
- the strike test is one mechanical read; a reviewer and the writer reach the same verdict
- the constraint row keeps the rule from overreach: a why-backed how survives

**cons**
- the peer lane adds one brain call to every vision stone, and one to every blueprint stone (F5, F7)
- on a light route, a criteria slip is caught one stage late, at the blueprint: the criteria yield is fixed after the fact (F7)
- `budget.tokens` grows by ~10–15%, on a boot every behaver session pays

**edge cases, and the pit of success**
- *wish with no how* → the table is empty; the rule asks naught (alterpath, itemized)
- *the how is the product* (a wish to **port** tool X) → X's observable contract is the contract; its internals are still a how. the why may name X ("replace X"); a requirement may not. each requirement states the observable behavior in its own words ("prints each account's week window"), never "as X does". so the strike test still holds: strike X, and every requirement still reads
- *over-correction* → the self-review checks the inverse: every verb the wisher named is still present (case=4)

## .open questions & assumptions

- [answered] **where the rule lives** — `behavior.wish/`: its subject is what a wish carries, though it binds vision and criteria (F2)
- [answered] **the name** — `trust-of-wisher-how`, not `…-implementations`: two of the four kinds the issue lists (a parity target, a done-when) are not implementations (F1)
- [answered] **does a light criteria guard exist?** — no. `getAllTemplatesBySize.ts:37-38` ships `2.1.criteria.blackbox.stone` in both sizes but its guard in `.heavy` only. so on a light route the criteria writer meets the rule through the cue and the stone line (F6), and a slip is blocked one stage on by the blueprint lane (F7)
- [answered] **is a peer lane on every vision and blueprint stone worth its brain call?** — yes, kept by the director: one call per stone costs less than a route rejected whole at review (F5, F7)
- [answered] **does a writer on a route always boot the said cue?** — yes: `init.behavior` is a behaver skill (`src/domain.roles/behaver/skills/init.behavior.sh`), so a repo that can create the route has behaver linked
- [answered] **what tests read the guard templates, and what does the new lane break?** — `initBehaviorDir.visionScope.integration.test.ts:206` pins the vision peer lanes to exactly `dimensional-decomposition` + `experience-coverage`; execution adds `wisher-how` there and asserts the lane holds the yield and each demo. the blueprint lane owes the same proof: a scope walk that shows it holds the criteria yield. `initBehaviorDir.integration.test.ts:104,143,184,217` assert extant slugs with `toContain`, so they hold; execution adds the new slug beside them
- [answered] **do extant routes gain the rule?** — the said cue, yes, at the next boot. the guard changes reach a route only at its init, or via `rhx route.guard.upgrade` (the templates' `provenance` line); extant routes keep their guards until then

## .what is awkward

- the rule lives under `behavior.wish/` yet fires at vision and criteria. the subject is the wish's content; the trigger is a later stage. a reader who browses `behavior.vision/` will not find it there
- "rough contract" is a judgment, not a test. the verbs are concrete; "rough shape" is not. the rule leans on the strike test to keep it honest
- the rule's blocker levels bite at vision and at blueprint, where the lanes run, not at criteria itself. a heavy route's criteria self-review catches the slip where it is made; a light route waits one stage
